# Malaria

## 1. Overview

Malaria is a parasitic disease. It involves high fevers, shaking chills, flu-like symptoms, and anemia (a condition in which the body does not have enough healthy red blood cells).

Malaria is caused by a parasite that is passed to humans by the bite of infected Anopheles mosquitoes. The parasites travel to the liver and then infect red blood cells.

Malaria is a major health problem in much of the tropics and subtropics. It is also a major disease hazard for travelers to warm climates. With treatment, the outcome is expected to be good in most cases of malaria. The outcome is poor in falciparum malaria with complications.

## 3. Causes and Disease Mechanism

### Cause of Malaria

Malaria is caused by a parasite. The parasite is passed to humans by the bite of infected Anopheles mosquitoes.

### How the Malaria Parasite Develops in the Body

After infection, malaria parasites in a form called sporozoites travel through the bloodstream to the liver. In the liver they mature and release another form of the parasite, called merozoites.

The merozoites enter the bloodstream and infect red blood cells (RBCs). The parasites multiply inside the red blood cells. The cells then break open within 48 to 72 hours and the released parasites infect more red blood cells.

### What Causes Malaria Symptoms

Most malaria symptoms are caused by:

- The release of merozoites into the bloodstream.
- Anemia resulting from the destruction of red blood cells.
- Large amounts of free hemoglobin being released into the blood after red blood cells break open. This can damage other organs, such as the kidneys.

### Resistance to Insecticides and Medicines

In some areas of the world, mosquitoes that carry malaria have developed resistance to insecticides. The provided source also states that the parasites have developed resistance to some "antibiotics". The term antibiotics is used in the source as written. Anti-malarial medicines are the drugs described elsewhere in the source for treating malaria. Which specific medicines are meant by this statement: Not specified in the provided source.

According to the provided source, these forms of resistance have made it hard to control both the rate of infection and the spread of malaria.

## 4. Risk Factors

Most people who live in areas where malaria is common have developed some immunity to the disease. Visitors to these areas do not have this immunity.

## 5. Signs and Symptoms

Not every person with malaria will have every symptom. The provided source lists these symptoms of malaria:

- Anemia
- Bloody stools
- Chills, fever, and sweating
- Coma
- Convulsions
- Headache
- Jaundice
- Muscle pain
- Nausea and vomiting

### Timing of Malaria Symptoms

The first symptoms of malaria usually occur 2 to 4 weeks after infection. They can appear as early as 8 days or as long as a year after infection. Malaria symptoms occur in cycles of 48 to 72 hours.

### Findings During a Physical Exam

During a physical exam, a health care provider may find an enlarged liver or an enlarged spleen in a person with malaria.

## 6. Disease Progression and Stages

### Progression Inside the Body

Malaria progresses through the parasite cycle described in the source. Parasites travel to the liver, mature, and release merozoites. The merozoites infect red blood cells, multiply, and break the cells open every 48 to 72 hours. This repeating cycle matches the 48 to 72 hour cycles of malaria symptoms.

### Incubation Period

The first symptoms of malaria usually occur 2 to 4 weeks after infection. They can appear as early as 8 days or as long as a year after infection.

### Outlook

The outcome of malaria is expected to be good in most cases with treatment. The outcome is poor in falciparum malaria with complications.

## 7. Transmission and Contagiousness

### Transmission by Mosquito Bite

Malaria is passed to humans by the bite of infected Anopheles mosquitoes.

### Other Ways Malaria Can Be Transmitted

Malaria can also be transmitted:

- From a mother to her unborn baby (congenital transmission).
- Through blood transfusions.

### Mosquitoes in Temperate Climates

Malaria can be carried by mosquitoes in temperate climates, but the parasite disappears over the winter.

## 9. Treatment and Medical Management

### Hospital Care

Malaria, especially falciparum malaria, is a medical emergency that requires a hospital stay.

### Chloroquine

Chloroquine is often used as an anti-malarial medicine. However, chloroquine-resistant malaria infections are common in some parts of the world.

### Treatments for Chloroquine-Resistant Malaria

The provided source lists these possible treatments for chloroquine-resistant infections:

- Artemisinin derivative combinations, including artemether and lumefantrine.
- Atovaquone-proguanil.
- A quinine-based regimen, in combination with doxycycline or clindamycin.
- Mefloquine, in combination with artesunate or doxycycline.

The choice of medicine depends, in part, on where the infection was acquired.

### Supportive Care

Medical care for malaria may include fluids given through a vein (IV), other medicines, and breathing (respiratory) support.

## 10. Prevention and Risk Reduction

### Preventive Medicines for Travelers

Visitors to areas where malaria is common do not have immunity and should take preventive medicines. A person should see a health care provider well before the trip. Preventive treatment may need to begin as long as 2 weeks before travel to the area and continue for a month after leaving the area.

The type of anti-malarial medicine prescribed depends on the area visited. Travelers to South America, Africa, the Indian subcontinent, Asia, and the South Pacific should take one of the following medicines:

- Mefloquine
- Doxycycline
- Chloroquine
- Hydroxychloroquine
- Atovaquone-proguanil

### Chloroquine and Resistance

Chloroquine has been the medicine of choice for protecting against malaria. Because of resistance, the provided source states it is now suggested only in areas where Plasmodium vivax, Plasmodium ovale, and Plasmodium malariae are present.

Falciparum malaria is becoming increasingly resistant to anti-malarial medicines. The provided source lists these recommended medicines for falciparum malaria prevention: mefloquine, atovaquone-proguanil (Malarone), and doxycycline.

### Preventive Medicines in Pregnancy

The provided source states that pregnant women should consider taking preventive medicines, because the risk to the fetus from the medicine is less than the risk of catching malaria. This decision should be made with a health care provider. The safety of each individual medicine in pregnancy: Not specified in the provided source.

### Preventing Mosquito Bites

Mosquito bites can be prevented by:

- Wearing protective clothing over the arms and legs.
- Using mosquito netting while sleeping.
- Using insect repellent.

## 11. Complications and Long-Term Effects

### Possible Complications

Health problems that may result from malaria include:

- Brain infection (cerebritis)
- Destruction of blood cells (hemolytic anemia)
- Kidney failure
- Liver failure
- Meningitis
- Respiratory failure from fluid in the lungs (pulmonary edema)
- Rupture of the spleen leading to massive internal bleeding (hemorrhage)

These are possible outcomes, not outcomes every person with malaria will have.

### Why Some Complications Occur

When red blood cells break open, large amounts of free hemoglobin are released into the blood. This can damage other organs, such as the kidneys.

### Outlook With Complications

The outcome is expected to be good in most cases of malaria with treatment. The outcome is poor in falciparum malaria with complications.

### Reducing the Risk of Complications
